# App UGC Playbook

App UGC = short-form video ads for mobile apps and games, running on TikTok, Meta, and (increasingly) YouTube Shorts. Native-feeling creator content that installs an app. The playbook here is what's converting on paid meta in 2026, not what looks good on a case study.

## Hooks that convert

- **"Apps I use to ___" listicle** — first 3–5 words name the outcome, not the category. "Apps I use to stop doomscrolling" beats "productivity apps I love."
- **Problem-callout** — "if you're still using [obvious competitor] you're behind." Names the pain, not the app, in the first line.
- **Pattern-interrupt visual** — start on a screen the viewer wasn't expecting (a broken calendar, a bank balance, a chat with mom). Curiosity gap opens before you speak.
- **Dupe / "the free version of ___"** — piggybacks on a well-known paid tool. Works because the frame is comparison, not pitch.
- **Transformation / receipt** — before-after screenshot in frame 1. Works even before audio starts. Best when the "after" is unbelievable enough to force a rewatch.
- **POV** — "POV: your competitor just launched this." Puts the viewer inside a scenario where they need the app to escape.
- **Authority stack** — "I've tried 30 note apps, this is the one." Numbers do the work; "30" is enough, don't say "hundreds."
- **Insider tell** — "the reason [category] apps are getting expensive." Feels like a leak, not a pitch. Works especially well for AI apps.

Avoid: "Have you ever ___?", "You NEED to try this app", any hook that ends in a question, any hook that names the app in the first line.

## The first 3 seconds

- **Frame 0**: face in shot OR pattern-interrupt visual, plus a 3–5 word text overlay. If your first frame is a logo, plain background, or slow zoom into a phone, kill it.
- **By 1.5s**: first hard cut. If the shot hasn't changed, retention drops off a cliff.
- **By 3s**: the promise is fully stated and the app has been visually referenced (screen recording in the corner, phone in hand, UI in frame). Do NOT save the reveal — that's a longer-form / organic move, not paid.
- **Text overlay** stays on screen the whole first 3s. Even if it repeats what you're saying — muted autoplay is the default state.

## Format & pacing

- **Aspect / length**: 9:16 vertical. 15–30s is the paid sweet spot; 30–45s only if the payoff justifies it. Under 12s reads as low-effort.
- **Cut cadence**: hard cut every 1.5–2.5s in the first half. Slow down after the reveal.
- **Camera**: handheld beats tripod for authenticity. Slight shake is a feature. Selfie POV outperforms third-person.
- **Audio**: your own voice, no compression, no music bed until the 8–12s mark (if at all). Silent-first shots with heavy text overlay are underused and work.
- **Music**: if you use it, pick a trending sound that's *not* on a Meta blocklist. Avoid instrumental "ad music" — it kills the native feel.
- **CTA**: soft, embedded in the last line. "Link's in bio" reads as ad. "It's called ___" or "search ___" reads as tip.

## Authentic vs "ad"

The line isn't about production quality — it's about who the video is *for*. UGC that works is made *for the viewer*, not for the brand.

Signals of "ad" (kill these):
- Logo intro / outro
- "This video is sponsored by"
- Ring-light glow, matching backdrop, staged desk
- The creator saying the app's tagline verbatim
- Uniform pacing throughout — no dynamic range
- Ending on the app icon fading in
- Any moment where the creator visibly reads a script

Signals of authentic (do these):
- Background clutter (unmade bed, kitchen counter, car interior)
- Umms, restarts, or a small self-correction left in
- Screen recording that has *other* apps visible (notifications, tabs)
- CTA that sounds like a rec to a friend
- The creator using the app *while filming*, not showing it after
- Face reacts on camera to the app doing something

## Common mistakes

- **Burying the app until 0:15+.** Kills install intent — most viewers already left. Show it by 0:03.
- **Over-explaining the feature set.** UGC pitches ONE outcome, not the whole app.
- **Using the brand's b-roll.** Instantly downgrades to "ad." Shoot your own even if it's worse.
- **Chasing a viral sound over the message.** Retention is fine but CTR to install collapses.
- **Ending with "download now."** Costs 30–50% CTR vs a native rec.
- **Vertical text down the side.** Reads as a designer's video, not a creator's.
- **Using the wrong platform's aesthetic.** TikTok-native cuts feel wrong on Meta; Reels edit style feels stiff on TikTok. Cut two versions.

## Current meta (2026)

- **AI app UGC is oversaturated.** The "AI app that does X" hook is worn — differentiate by outcome, not by "it's AI."
- **Dupe / free-alternative angle** is the strongest hook for productivity, design, and creative-tool apps right now.
- **Split-screen "which one wins"** is spiking for gaming and utility apps.
- **Faceless UGC with heavy text overlay** is winning for finance, health, and gambling-adjacent verticals where creator identity is a liability.
- **Native TikTok watermarks (real or spoofed)** on Meta ads is winning CTR — the "leaked from TikTok" feel outperforms clean edits.
- **Trend-jacking is dead for direct-response.** Riding a sound only works if the message is *entirely* different from the trend's original context — otherwise you look like a follower.
- **UGC creators are shifting to Spark Ads / whitelisted formats.** If you're shooting for a brand, ask about whitelisting — it 2–3× your rate and unlocks better placements.
- **App Store creative is bleeding into UGC** — the first frame is now often optimized to match the app's screenshot 1, giving a subconscious "I've seen this before" hit when they land on the store page.
