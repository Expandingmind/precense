# Faceless Creator Playbook

Faceless = short-form where the creator's identity is deliberately absent. Voiceover + b-roll, screen recordings, AI-generated visuals, or on-screen text as the whole frame. Common in finance, self-improvement, geopolitics, mystery, food, and increasingly in AI/tech commentary.

## Hooks that convert

- **Claim + delta** — "This costs $19 and it's built with $0.30 of parts." Number + surprise.
- **Countdown / rank** — "The 5th one is illegal in 6 states." Delayed payoff, forced watch-through.
- **"Nobody's talking about ___"** — insider framing. Works because faceless format already feels like a leak.
- **Historical rewind** — "In 1997, this photo was banned." Faceless format is trusted for archival / documentary energy.
- **Text-only cold open** — first 2s is a single sentence on a black or muted background. Highest completion rates for finance and tech content.
- **AI voice + AI visuals** for meta / gaming / crypto — audience expects it, doesn't punish it. Anywhere else it reads as spam.

## The first 3 seconds

- **Voiceover starts at 0:00.** No music intro, no title card. Delay in the voice = drop-off.
- **Visual is already moving.** Static image = swipe. Kill zoom-ins that take 1s+.
- **The frame teaches the format.** If it's a listicle, the number is visible. If it's a countdown, the counter is on screen. Never leave the viewer wondering what kind of video this is.
- **Text overlay ≠ the voiceover.** They should carry complementary information — otherwise you're wasting a channel.

## Format & pacing

- **9:16 vertical, 45–90s** is the strong zone (longer than face-to-cam because there's no fatigue on a person).
- **B-roll changes every 1–2s.** Even if it's stock, the movement is what holds attention.
- **Voiceover is 155–180 wpm.** Slower = boring, faster = losing non-native speakers.
- **Music bed at low volume** (start at -18dB, duck under VO). Trending sounds hurt more than help here — they signal "personal" content and clash with the objective tone.
- **Captions are non-optional.** Word-by-word or 2–3 word chunks, not full-sentence blocks.
- **CTA is a follow-up hook**: "part 2 in comments" or "the answer's in my next video" — faceless creators live on multi-part retention.

## Authentic vs "ad"

The trust problem for faceless is *sourcelessness* — no face means no accountability. Compensate:

Signals that build trust:
- Cite sources on screen (chyrons, "source: [outlet]", timestamps)
- Screen recordings of primary material (docs, articles, real UI)
- Consistent voice across videos — even AI voice is fine if it's the same one every time
- A named channel identity (a persona, a mascot, a running visual motif)

Signals that flag "spam / farm":
- Stock voice that changes across videos
- Random uncredited stock b-roll
- No channel identity beyond a logo
- Text overlay in generic fonts (Impact, Arial Black without customization)
- Music that sounds like a stock library motion-graphics track

## Common mistakes

- **Using AI voice for emotional content.** Works for informational, dies for storytelling.
- **B-roll that doesn't advance the point.** Filler shots kill watch time; every clip should visually earn its second on screen.
- **Full-sentence captions.** Reading fatigue → swipe.
- **Long intros with the channel logo.** Cut them entirely.
- **Copy-paste script structure across videos.** The algorithm learns your template and stops pushing.
- **Uploading the same video across TikTok/Reels/Shorts.** Each platform's watermark detection now down-ranks cross-posts. Re-cut or re-edit.

## Current meta (2026)

- **AI-generated visuals** (Sora, Veo, Kling) are the dominant b-roll for tech, finance, and lifestyle-explainer faceless. Audience-tolerant when the topic is speculative or historical; less tolerant when the topic is present-day and factual.
- **Text-only videos** with heavy motion typography are winning in finance and business commentary — the "no distractions, just information" positioning.
- **Real archival / photo-based faceless** is quietly outperforming AI visuals for documentary and mystery — the "found footage" feel converts.
- **Multi-part hooks** ("this is part 1 of 4") force channel follows in a way single videos don't.
- **Longer faceless (60–120s)** is beating 30s cuts on Reels — Meta's algorithm is pushing longer watch time.
- **Screen-recording faceless** (only shows a phone/laptop) is dominating anything tool-related, AI product reviews, and "how I made $X" content.
