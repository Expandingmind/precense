# Founder / B2B Playbook

Founders, operators, and B2B creators building an audience *through* short-form. The math: one post that reaches the right 500 people beats one that reaches 500K wrong people. Everything below is optimized for that.

## Hooks that convert

- **Contrarian call** — "Everyone raising a seed round right now is making the same mistake." Names a category, positions you as inside it.
- **Numbered receipt** — "We got to $2M ARR in 11 months. Here's what didn't work." Number in first 4 words, receipt (loss / mistake / cut) as the promise.
- **Insider vocabulary** — one term only your target audience uses. "PLG onboarding is dead." "Your ICP isn't who you think." Filters non-buyers out.
- **Firing the client / niching down** — "I stopped taking clients under $50k. Here's what happened." Confident enough to alienate — filters for peer buyers.
- **Category redefinition** — "This isn't sales, this is packaging." Reframe as service to your buyer's real problem.
- **Screenshotted dm / customer quote** — pastes a real interaction as the cold open. Feels leaked, not staged.
- **"I was wrong about ___"** — costs zero to say, buys enormous authority. Only works if the reversal is specific.

Avoid: "3 tips for ___", "Here's how I ___", any hook that ends in a question, any hook that starts with your own name.

## The first 3 seconds

- **Face + eye contact + text overlay** in frame 0. The text is the hook headline in ≤7 words.
- **Speak the hook, don't read it.** If it sounds rehearsed, you're already behind. One take, natural cadence.
- **No preamble.** "Hey guys, welcome back to my channel" costs 40% of your retention. Cut it.
- **Location does work.** Filming from an office (real, not staged), a factory floor, an event, or in motion (walking, driving with hands-free) reads as busy operator, not full-time creator.
- **Setting the frame at 0:00:** if the video is a story, first line is the punchline. If it's an argument, first line is the claim. If it's a teach, first line is the outcome.

## Format & pacing

- **9:16 vertical is default** but 1:1 outperforms on LinkedIn, and 16:9 landscape works for YouTube-native founders. Pick your primary channel, cut for it, repurpose after.
- **45–90s** is the founder-content zone. Under 30s reads shallow; over 2m reads self-indulgent unless it's a story.
- **Cut every 3–5s** on face-to-cam. Slower than UGC because the trust builds through eye contact and continuous speech.
- **B-roll / product cutaways** at moments of highest specificity — the number, the receipt, the chart, the dashboard.
- **Captions are mandatory.** Two-line max, high contrast, sans-serif. Anything with a "creator" font (handwriting, bubble) reads as consumer-brand and loses B2B trust.
- **CTA is a "next step" not a "buy now."** "DM me the word [term]" beats "book a call." Even better: end on a hook that forces a comment ("what would you have done?").

## Authentic vs "ad"

For founder content, the risk isn't looking like an ad — it's looking like a *ghostwritten LinkedIn post read aloud*. The trust signals:

Signals of real:
- Specific numbers (not "6-figures", not "millions" — "$847k")
- Naming real people, tools, competitors (even critically)
- Admitting a mistake that costs you something to admit
- Filming somewhere that shows the work (office, factory, event, backstage)
- Interrupting yourself to add nuance
- Referencing recent events / this week / yesterday

Signals of "content marketing":
- Overly clean framing, ring-light, backdrop matching your brand colors
- Recycled advice ("focus on your customer!")
- Vague success ("we scaled fast")
- Repeated brand mentions
- A tagline in the caption
- Reading directly from a teleprompter you can see your eyes tracking

## Common mistakes

- **Pitching in the video.** The video's job is filtering + credibility. The DM / profile is where you pitch.
- **Speaking to "everyone."** If a marketing manager could nod along, so could a designer, so could a random college kid — you've said nothing.
- **Ending on "follow for more."** Costs 20%+ engagement vs a question that provokes comments.
- **Trying to go viral.** Founder content that goes broadly viral is often the wrong audience — your ICP is a slim vertical slice. Prefer 5k views + 40 DMs over 500k views + 2 DMs.
- **Batch shooting.** Shows in the eyes — five videos in the same outfit reads as content mill.
- **Long stories with no receipt.** If the story doesn't end in a number, a decision, or a lesson, cut it in half.
- **Filming vertically with laptop-quality audio.** Get a lav mic. It's the single highest-ROI upgrade.

## Current meta (2026)

- **LinkedIn is now the primary distribution channel for B2B founders** — LinkedIn's video algorithm is generous, the audience is bought-in, and comments outperform every other network. Instagram is second, TikTok is third, X is a lottery.
- **The "founder journey / building in public" format** is oversaturated. What's working now: **operator posts** — a specific decision, tradeoff, or hire, told with the receipt attached.
- **Documentation-style content** (walking through your dashboard, showing a real slack thread, screen-recording your CRM) is winning for early-stage founders — it teaches AND filters at the same time.
- **The "day-in-the-life" format is dead** for founders unless the day involves something rare (a factory tour, a deal signing, a fundraise close).
- **Multi-guest content** (bringing a customer, an ex-competitor, or a public operator onto your video) 3–5×'s reach — the guest's audience shows up.
- **Podcast clips are undervalued** — a 90s cut from a real conversation outperforms a 90s solo take because the two-person dynamic reads as genuine.
- **AI content is a red flag for B2B buyers.** AI voice, AI b-roll, or obvious AI-written captions on a founder's page destroy trust. This is the one niche where "handmade" is still a competitive advantage.
